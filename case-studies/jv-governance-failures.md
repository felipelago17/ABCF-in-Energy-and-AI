# JV Governance Failures & Corruption Pathways

**Summary.** Joint ventures and minority stakes are the single most dangerous
ABCF structure, because they combine **high-value, high-specificity assets** with
**diffuse monitoring incentives** and **shared-but-unclear responsibility**. This
study generalises the failure architecture common to the JV/consortium cases in
this repository (Bonny Island/TSKJ, and the intermediary-to-SOE patterns in the
energy and UK studies) into a reusable diagnostic — and the remediation that
answers each failure mode. It is analytical; the underlying facts are cited in
the linked case files.

## Why JVs are structurally hazardous

Reading through the transaction-cost lens (see
[`../institutional/transaction-cost-economics.md`](../institutional/transaction-cost-economics.md)):
- **Asset specificity** — JV capital is sunk and immobile, creating hold-up
  leverage for counterparties and officials.
- **Diffuse monitoring incentives** — with several owners, each may assume
  another is watching (the free-rider/diffusion problem).
- **Shared control, unclear accountability** — operatorship, board seats, and
  reserved matters are negotiated, so *who is responsible for compliance* is
  often ambiguous by construction.
- **A separate legal vehicle** executes transactions — the JV can bribe on behalf
  of its owners while each owner's own controls never touch the payment.
- **Local partners at the state interface** — JVs frequently exist *because* a
  local partner manages government relationships, i.e., the highest-risk function.

## The failure architecture (five recurring gaps)

| # | Failure mode | What it looks like | Polycentric reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Coverage gap** | The JV's payment flows are watched by no owner's compliance function | A high-risk interface with no assigned centre |
| 2 | **Diffusion of responsibility** | Every partner assumes another oversees compliance | Multiple centres, none acting |
| 3 | **Captured local partner/agent** | The partner managing officials is the corruption vehicle | A gatekeeper co-opted by the scheme |
| 4 | **Incentive misalignment** | A partner profits from the tainted contract and has no reason to police it | A centre with visibility but no incentive |
| 5 | **Delegation without control rights** | Owner delegates operatorship but retains no audit/information rights | Responsibility exported without the tools to exercise it |

These map directly onto the failure modes in
[`../institutional/polycentric-governance.md`](../institutional/polycentric-governance.md).

## Board-level red flags in JVs

- Compliance responsibility for the JV **not assigned** to any specific owner or
  role.
- A local partner introduced or required by an **official/customer**, with equity
  in lieu of a disclosed fee.
- **No visibility** into JV disbursements to third parties/intermediaries.
- **Minority stake** with cash-flow exposure but no governance/audit rights.
- Operatorship **delegated** without retained information or audit rights.
- JV partner UBO **unresolved** or resolving to a PEP (see
  [`../institutional/beneficial-ownership.md`](../institutional/beneficial-ownership.md)).

## Remediation architecture (control by failure mode)

Design these into the JV *ex ante* — they are far cheaper before capital is sunk:

| Failure mode | Control |
|---|---|
| Coverage gap | Assign a **named owner/role** accountable for JV compliance; require **payment-flow visibility** into third-party disbursements |
| Diffusion of responsibility | A **JV-level compliance program** and committee with defined reporting lines; explicit allocation in the JV agreement |
| Captured partner/agent | **EDD and UBO resolution** on partners; **audit rights** over the partner's state-facing dealings; disclosed-agent and no-undisclosed-sub-agent clauses |
| Incentive misalignment | **Reserved matters**, co-approval of high-risk payments, and **termination/exit** rights on findings |
| Delegation without control | Retain **information and audit rights** proportionate to the stake and risk — never delegate operatorship without them |

See the [JV checkpoints](../risk-tools/due-diligence-checklists.md) and
[`../compliance/board-management-certification.md`](../compliance/board-management-certification.md)
(flow-down obligations, audit rights, reps/warranties).

## The transaction-cost prescription

JVs are the paradigm case for **hybrid governance**: too high-risk and
relationship-specific for market screening alone, but jointly owned so pure
hierarchy is unavailable. The efficient response is a **hybrid** — reserved
matters, JV-level compliance, co-approval, audit rights, and credible exit —
proportioned to the sunk stakes. Spending here is justified precisely because the
expected loss (penalties, disgorgement, unwinding a sunk-capital project,
debarment) is largest exactly where the JV structure concentrates risk (see
[`../institutional/transaction-cost-economics.md`](../institutional/transaction-cost-economics.md)).

## Lessons

1. **Assign compliance accountability explicitly** — ambiguity is the default and
   the danger.
2. **Never delegate without control rights** — retain audit and information rights
   proportionate to exposure.
3. **See the money** — visibility into JV third-party disbursements is the single
   highest-value control.
4. **Resolve partner UBO before committing** — unresolved ownership in a JV is a
   blocking condition, not a footnote.
5. **Build exit and reserved matters ex ante** — remediation levers are cheap
   before capital is sunk and near-impossible after.
