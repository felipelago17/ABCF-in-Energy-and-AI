# FCPA Enforcement in Energy

**Summary.** Energy is the most heavily-enforced FCPA sector because it
concentrates every risk factor: state-owned counterparties (whose employees are
"foreign officials"), enormous sunk investments (hold-up leverage), long
project durations, high-risk jurisdictions, and heavy intermediary use. These
studies read landmark matters as governance-network failures. **All figures are
cited to primary sources; verify before relying on them.** See the
[misattribution notes](README.md) for corrections to commonly-garbled versions
of these cases.

---

## Bonny Island LNG — the TSKJ joint venture (KBR/Halliburton)

- **Resolution:** 2009. **Authorities:** DOJ (criminal) + SEC (civil).
- **Amount:** **$579M total** — **$402M** DOJ criminal fine (KBR) + **$177M**
  SEC disgorgement (Halliburton, which indemnified). At the time a record FCPA
  resolution against a U.S. company.
- **Statute:** FCPA anti-bribery and related charges.
- **Sector:** Oil & gas (LNG construction).
- **Conduct:** The **TSKJ** consortium (KBR, Technip, Snamprogetti/Saipem, JGC)
  paid **more than $180M** through intermediaries — notably Jeffrey Tesler — to
  Nigerian officials to win engineering-procurement-construction contracts for
  the Bonny Island liquefied-natural-gas project.

> ⚠️ This is **not** a Shell case. Shell was a shareholder in the project's
> owner (Nigeria LNG Ltd.), not a TSKJ defendant. See [README](README.md).

**Governance-network reading.** The JV structure diffused responsibility: a
consortium vehicle executed the bribery, and each parent could treat oversight
as someone else's job. The intermediary (Tesler) sat at the state interface with
no effective principal-side visibility of where his money went — the classic
asset-specificity/opportunism failure (see
[`../institutional/transaction-cost-economics.md`](../institutional/transaction-cost-economics.md)).
Detection ultimately came from *outside* the JV's own governance, the hallmark
of a coverage gap (see [`jv-governance-failures.md`](jv-governance-failures.md)).

## Weatherford International

- **Resolution:** **2013** (November). **Authorities:** **DOJ, SEC, OFAC, and
  BIS** — a four-agency resolution.
- **Amount:** **~$253M total**, including a then-record **$50M BIS** penalty.
- **Statute:** **FCPA** (DOJ charged the internal-controls provision; SEC charged
  anti-bribery, books-and-records, and internal-controls) **plus** export-control
  and sanctions violations (OFAC/BIS).
- **Sector:** Oilfield services.
- **Conduct:** Deficient internal accounting controls enabled bribery across
  multiple countries; separately, the company violated U.S. export controls and
  sanctions.

**Governance-network reading.** Weatherford is the paradigm **convergence** case:
one company, one weak-controls root cause, exposure under corruption *and*
sanctions/export regimes simultaneously — resolved by four authorities at once.
It is the enforcement-side proof of the argument in
[`../compliance/sanctions-abcf-alignment.md`](../compliance/sanctions-abcf-alignment.md):
the internal-controls failure that lets bribes through is often the same failure
that lets sanctioned trade through. The remedy is a *unified* controls
architecture, not siloed compliance functions.

## Shell — the 2010 Panalpina-related settlement

- **Resolution:** **2010** (November). **Authorities:** DOJ + SEC.
- **Amount:** **~$48M total** — **$30M** DOJ criminal penalty (deferred
  prosecution) + **~$18.1M** SEC disgorgement and prejudgment interest.
- **Statute:** FCPA anti-bribery and books-and-records.
- **Sector:** Oil & gas (customs/freight-forwarding).
- **Conduct:** Part of the broader **Panalpina** sweep (seven companies, ~$236.5M
  combined). Shell's subsidiary SNEPCO paid ~$2M to subcontractors on the Bonga
  deepwater project knowing the funds would be passed by freight forwarder
  Panalpina as bribes to Nigerian customs officials to import equipment.

**Governance-network reading.** The customs interface is a recurring bribe
chokepoint in energy logistics. Detection came from an *industry-wide* DOJ/SEC
sweep, not from any single company's controls — a demonstration that the
polycentric system sometimes surfaces conduct through cross-firm pattern
detection that no single firm's compliance function would catch.

> ⚠️ There is **no verifiable Shell "2016 ~$105M FCPA" settlement**, and Shell
> was **not** charged in the **OPL 245** matter (DOJ closed without charges;
> Italian acquittals in **March 2021**). See [README](README.md).

## Modern commodities-trading enforcement (2022–2024)

A wave of resolutions confirms that commodities/oil trading remains a top FCPA
risk sector — and that penalties have grown by an order of magnitude:

- **Glencore (2022):** guilty plea; **over $1.1B** in U.S. penalties. FCPA
  bribery (2007–2018) to officials in Nigeria, Cameroon, Côte d'Ivoire,
  Equatorial Guinea, DRC, Venezuela, and Brazil to obtain oil from state
  entities. Coordinated with the UK (SFO) and Brazil.
- **Gunvor S.A. (2024):** guilty plea; **over $661M** ($374.56M criminal fine +
  $287.14M forfeiture). FCPA — over **$97M** paid via intermediaries (2012–2020)
  to bribe Ecuadorian officials (Ministry of Hydrocarbons / Petroecuador) for oil
  contracts.
- **Albemarle (2023):** **~$218.5M** (DOJ non-prosecution agreement + SEC). FCPA
  — third-party/subsidiary bribery of officials in Vietnam, Indonesia, and India
  (2009–2017) in the refining-catalyst business.

**Governance-network reading.** All three run through **intermediaries** at the
state/SOE interface — precisely the highest-risk cell of the
[risk matrix](../risk-tools/abcf-risk-matrix.md). The scale of the penalties
reflects both aggregated multi-year conduct and coordinated cross-border
resolution (see [`../governance-resources/enforcement-coordination.md`](../governance-resources/enforcement-coordination.md)).
The recurring lesson: intermediary due diligence, payment controls, and audit
rights are the controls that would most have changed these outcomes.

## Cross-cutting lessons for energy ABCF

1. **The state interface is the risk.** SOE counterparties + intermediaries +
   high-risk jurisdictions is the modal fact pattern. Score and control it as
   High (see [risk matrix](../risk-tools/abcf-risk-matrix.md)).
2. **JV/consortium structures diffuse oversight.** Design explicit compliance
   rights and payment-flow visibility into the vehicle (see
   [`jv-governance-failures.md`](jv-governance-failures.md)).
3. **Controls failures converge.** The same weakness invites bribery *and*
   sanctions/export breaches — unify the program.
4. **Detection often comes from outside.** Industry sweeps, whistleblowers, and
   coordinated cross-border action surface what internal controls miss — build
   for that reality.
