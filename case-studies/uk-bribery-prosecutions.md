# UK Bribery Act Prosecutions

**Summary.** UK enforcement illustrates three governance lessons: the **DPA** as
a coordinated-resolution tool (Rolls-Royce), the limits of enforcement and the
power of **legal privilege** (ENRC), and the **convergence** of corruption with
other misconduct (Société Générale). **Verify figures against the primary
sources cited.** See the [misattribution notes](README.md) — the ENRC matter in
particular is widely mischaracterised.

---

## Rolls-Royce (2017) — the landmark SFO DPA

- **Resolution:** **January 2017.** **Authorities:** UK **SFO** (Deferred
  Prosecution Agreement, approved by Sir Brian Leveson), U.S. **DOJ** (DPA), and
  Brazil's **MPF** (leniency agreement).
- **Amount:** **£497M** (plus SFO costs) UK portion; **~$170M (£141M)** DOJ
  criminal penalty; **~$26M** Brazil. **Global total ≈ £671M (~$800M).**
- **Statute:** UK — conspiracy to corrupt / **failure to prevent bribery**;
  U.S. — conspiracy to violate the **FCPA**.
- **Sector:** Aerospace and industrial engineering (civil aerospace, defence,
  energy/power systems).
- **Conduct:** Used intermediaries to pay bribes (conduct spanning ~1989–2013) to
  win contracts across China, India, Indonesia, Malaysia, Nigeria, Russia,
  Thailand, and elsewhere.

**Governance-network reading.** Rolls-Royce is the model **coordinated
resolution**: three authorities, one factual record, allocated penalties — the
anti-piling-on logic in action (see
[`../governance-resources/enforcement-coordination.md`](../governance-resources/enforcement-coordination.md)).
It is also a **Section 7** exemplar: liability flowed from **associated persons**
(intermediaries) acting for the company, and the resolution turned on the
adequacy — or inadequacy — of the company's preventive procedures (see
[`../frameworks/uk-bribery-act.md`](../frameworks/uk-bribery-act.md)). The
enormous intermediary network across high-risk jurisdictions is a textbook
third-party-risk failure (see
[`../institutional/vendor-third-party-risk.md`](../institutional/vendor-third-party-risk.md)).

## ENRC — investigation, privilege, and the limits of enforcement

- **Status:** The SFO opened a criminal investigation in **2013** into alleged
  fraud, bribery, and corruption around ENRC's acquisitions in Africa and
  Kazakhstan. It ran for over a decade and produced **no criminal conviction of
  the company.**
- **The famous case law is about privilege, not guilt:** *SFO v ENRC* **[2018]
  EWCA Civ 2006** — the **Court of Appeal (September 2018)** held that documents
  from ENRC's internal investigation were protected by **litigation privilege**.
  This is a landmark privilege precedent, and the SFO did not appeal.
- **Related civil litigation:** *ENRC v Dechert & Neil Gerrard* **[2022] EWHC
  1138 (Comm)** — the High Court found ENRC's former law firm and a partner
  liable in connection with the conduct of the investigation (including
  unauthorised disclosures to the SFO). This is **civil** litigation about the
  investigation's conduct — **not** a bribery conviction.
- **Sector:** Mining and metals.

> ⚠️ **ENRC was not convicted of bribery.** Do not present the matter as a
> conviction. See [README](README.md).

**Governance-network reading.** ENRC is a study in **enforcement limits and the
gatekeeper problem**. Two lessons: (1) **legal privilege** shapes what
regulators can see, constraining one detection channel and putting a premium on
the others (auditors, whistleblowers, transparency data); and (2) the
**internal-investigation function itself can fail** — the civil litigation
turned on the conduct of the very advisers meant to be a control. A gatekeeper
that malfunctions is a captured/failed centre in polycentric terms (see
[`../institutional/polycentric-governance.md`](../institutional/polycentric-governance.md)).

## Société Générale (2018) — corruption converging with market abuse

- **Resolution:** **June 2018.** **Authorities:** U.S. **DOJ** (guilty plea by a
  subsidiary), France's **PNF** (Parquet National Financier), and U.S. **CFTC**.
- **Amount:** **over $860M** in combined U.S./France criminal penalties —
  **$585M for the Libya bribery** scheme + **$275M for LIBOR** manipulation; with
  additional CFTC penalties/disgorgement for LIBOR, total exposure exceeded
  **$1B**.
- **Statute:** **FCPA** (Libya bribery) + commodities/benchmark (LIBOR)
  manipulation.
- **Sector:** Banking / financial services.
- **Conduct:** Paid a Libyan intermediary **over $90M**, portions of which
  reached Gaddafi-era Libyan officials, to secure ~$3.66B in investments from
  Libyan state institutions (earning ~$523M in profit); separately manipulated
  LIBOR submissions.

**Governance-network reading.** Two lessons. First, **convergence**: one
institution, two distinct misconduct types (bribery + market manipulation),
resolved together — governance that watches only one is blind to the other.
Second, the **intermediary-to-SOE** pattern is identical to the energy cases:
value routed through an agent to state-institution officials, with the principal
lacking visibility of the money's destination.

## Cross-cutting lessons for UK-nexus ABCF

1. **Section 7 makes your program your defence.** Associated-person liability is
   near-strict; only "adequate procedures" (s.7(2)) protect you — so build and
   evidence them (see [`../compliance/board-management-certification.md`](../compliance/board-management-certification.md)).
2. **Coordinate, don't duplicate.** DPAs and cross-border coordination reward
   single, cooperative global resolution.
3. **Privilege shapes detection.** With one channel constrained, invest in the
   others (audit, whistleblowing, monitoring).
4. **Gatekeepers can fail.** Internal-investigation and adviser functions are
   controls that themselves need oversight.
