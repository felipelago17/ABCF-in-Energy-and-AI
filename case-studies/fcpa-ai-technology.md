# FCPA in AI & Technology

**Summary.** There is **no frontier-AI-specific FCPA resolution as of 2026.** The
relevant precedents are **enterprise-software / IT vendors**, whose fact
patterns transfer directly to AI/compute procurement: sales to state-owned
entities through local partners and distributors, with value routed via
discounts, commissions, and "marketing" payments. This study uses those cases to
build an *anticipatory* risk model for AI vendors — see
[`../ai-compute/abcf-ai-procurement.md`](../ai-compute/abcf-ai-procurement.md).
**Do not represent any of the below as an "AI" case.** Verify all figures against
the linked primary sources.

---

## SAP SE (2024) — the closest technology precedent

- **Resolution:** **January 2024.** **Authorities:** DOJ (three-year deferred
  prosecution agreement) + SEC.
- **Amount:** **~$220M** total — DOJ **$118.8M** criminal fine + ~**$103M**
  forfeiture (partly offset by SEC disgorgement); SEC ~**$100M** disgorgement and
  interest.
- **Statute:** FCPA anti-bribery **and** books-and-records/internal-controls.
- **Sector:** Enterprise software.
- **Conduct:** Improper payments (2013–2018) to officials at state-owned entities
  in **South Africa and Indonesia** to obtain software and services contracts,
  routed through third-party intermediaries.

**Why it transfers to AI/compute.** SAP's pattern — **software/technology sold
to state-owned entities via local intermediaries, with bribes disguised as
legitimate commercial payments** — is exactly the structure a sovereign or SOE
**compute/AI-infrastructure** deal would take. The counterparties (SOEs) make
the staff foreign officials; the intermediaries create the information asymmetry;
the "discount/commission/marketing" line items are where value leaks.

## Earlier software-vendor precedents (pattern confirmation)

The same fact pattern recurs across the technology sector's FCPA history
(examples; verify specifics against SEC/DOJ records before citing figures):
- **Microsoft (2019)** — SEC resolution concerning improper payments/discount
  schemes through partners in multiple countries.
- **Oracle (2022)** — SEC resolution concerning slush funds financed via excessive
  discounts to channel partners.

**The common mechanism:** the **channel/partner discount** as a corruption
vehicle. Inflated discounts or rebates to distributors create off-book pools
that fund improper payments — a books-and-records and internal-controls failure
even where the anti-bribery proof is indirect. This is why the FCPA accounting
provisions are the workhorse in tech cases (see
[`../frameworks/fcpa.md`](../frameworks/fcpa.md)).

## Anticipatory model for AI / compute vendors

Mapping the software precedents onto AI/compute procurement:

| Software-case mechanism | AI/compute analogue | Control |
|---|---|---|
| Sale to SOE via local partner | Sovereign/SOE compute or data-centre deal via local integrator | UBO-resolve & EDD the integrator |
| Inflated channel discounts → slush fund | Rebates/credits/allocation preferences → off-book value | Payment & discount-approval controls; books-and-records discipline |
| "Marketing"/"consulting" line items | "Enablement"/"capacity"/"advisory" payments | Deliverable-matched invoicing; audit rights |
| Distributor obscures end-customer | Integrator obscures end-user of controlled compute | Unified corruption + **export-control** end-user diligence |

The AI-specific twist is the **export-control/sanctions overlay** on advanced
computing: the same intermediary that could disguise a bribe could disguise a
restricted end-user, making the corruption and BIS/OFAC screens inseparable (see
[`../ai-compute/frontier-ai-enforcement.md`](../ai-compute/frontier-ai-enforcement.md)
and [`../compliance/sanctions-abcf-alignment.md`](../compliance/sanctions-abcf-alignment.md)).

## Lessons

1. **Channel partners are the risk surface.** Discounts, rebates, and
   commissions to distributors/integrators are the classic corruption vehicle —
   control them with approval limits, deliverable-matched invoicing, and audit
   rights.
2. **Accounting controls do the work.** Books-and-records/internal-controls
   charges resolve most tech cases; disciplined records are frontline defence.
3. **Add the export-control lens for AI.** Advanced-compute deals need a unified
   corruption + sanctions/export screen keyed on one UBO/end-user determination.
4. **Do not overclaim.** There is no AI-specific FCPA case yet — argue from
   analogy, and keep the [misattribution notes](README.md) accurate as this space
   develops.
