# International & Sectoral ABCF Standards

**Summary.** Beyond national criminal statutes (FCPA, UK Bribery Act), a layer
of **international conventions** and **voluntary/sectoral standards** shapes
ABCF governance. These are the connective tissue of a polycentric system: they
create shared expectations, certification signals, and transparency
infrastructure that national enforcers, investors, and counterparties rely on.
None is self-enforcing — each works through the institutions that adopt it.

## Map of instruments

| Instrument | Type | Binding? | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| UNCAC | Treaty (UN) | On states parties | Criminalisation, prevention, asset recovery, mutual assistance |
| OECD Anti-Bribery Convention | Treaty (OECD) | On parties | Criminalise foreign bribery; peer review (Working Group on Bribery) |
| ISO 37001 | Management-system standard | Voluntary, certifiable | Anti-bribery management system (ABMS) requirements |
| EITI Standard | Multi-stakeholder standard | Voluntary (by country/company) | Extractives revenue & beneficial-ownership transparency |
| ICMM Mining Principles | Sector membership commitment | Voluntary (members) | Responsible-mining performance expectations |

## UNCAC — UN Convention Against Corruption

The only near-universal anti-corruption treaty. It obliges states parties to
criminalise bribery (of national and foreign officials), embezzlement, and
laundering; to adopt **preventive** measures (public-sector integrity,
procurement transparency, whistleblower protection); and to cooperate on
**asset recovery** and mutual legal assistance. Compliance is assessed through
the **Implementation Review Mechanism (IRM)** — a peer-review process that
produces country reports. For governance analysis, UNCAC matters less as
enforceable law than as the framework that *harmonises* national regimes and
underwrites cross-border cooperation. See
[`governance-resources/multi-stakeholder-initiatives.md`](../governance-resources/multi-stakeholder-initiatives.md).

## OECD Anti-Bribery Convention

Focused specifically on the **supply side** of foreign bribery. Its distinctive
mechanism is the **Working Group on Bribery (WGB)** peer-review process, which
publicly rates each party's implementation and enforcement. The WGB's Phase
reports are a genuine accountability lever — states are named for
under-enforcement. The Convention is the treaty backbone behind the FCPA's and
UK Bribery Act's foreign-bribery focus.

## ISO 37001 — Anti-Bribery Management Systems

A **certifiable** management-system standard (aligned with the ISO high-level
structure shared by ISO 9001, 14001, 27001). It specifies requirements for an
anti-bribery management system: leadership and policy, bribery risk assessment,
due diligence, financial and non-financial controls, whistleblowing,
investigation, and continual improvement.

- **What certification signals.** ISO 37001 certification demonstrates that an
  organisation has *implemented a system*, audited by a third party. It is a
  procurement and counterparty **signal** — useful in vendor vetting.
- **What it does not do.** Certification does not prove the absence of bribery,
  and regulators (notably the U.S. DOJ) have cautioned that certification is
  **not a safe harbour** — programs are judged on substance and effectiveness,
  not the certificate. Treat ISO 37001 as evidence of design, not a defence.

It maps naturally onto both the DOJ compliance-program factors and the UK
"adequate procedures" principles — an organisation building for one is largely
building for all three.

## EITI — Extractive Industries Transparency Initiative

A multi-stakeholder standard (governments, companies, civil society) requiring
disclosure of the payment flows between extractive companies and states —
licences, production, revenues, and, increasingly, **beneficial ownership** of
extractive licence-holders. EITI attacks corruption at its highest-risk
interface (state resource revenues) through **transparency infrastructure**
rather than prosecution. For energy-sector ABCF it is a primary data source and
a governance-network node connecting host states, companies, and watchdogs. See
[`institutional/beneficial-ownership.md`](../institutional/beneficial-ownership.md).

## ICMM Mining Principles & IPIECA

- **ICMM (International Council on Mining & Metals)** — membership commits mining
  and metals companies to performance expectations, including anti-corruption
  and transparency, with validated assurance.
- **IPIECA** — the global oil-and-gas industry association for environmental and
  social issues, which produces anti-corruption and transparency guidance for
  the petroleum sector.

These sector bodies function as **private ordering**: peer commitments and
assurance that raise the floor across an industry without waiting for
legislation. See
[`governance-resources/industry-standards-bodies.md`](../governance-resources/industry-standards-bodies.md).

## How the layers interact (the governance point)

National statutes provide **credible punishment**; conventions provide
**harmonisation and cooperation**; certifiable standards provide **verifiable
design**; sectoral commitments provide **peer pressure and assurance**; and
transparency initiatives provide the **information** that makes detection
possible. Corruption prevention emerges from the *overlap*, not from any single
layer — the core polycentric thesis of this repository.
