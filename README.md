# ABCF Governance: Anti-Bribery, Corruption & Fraud Prevention Frameworks

A live regulatory intelligence and institutional analysis repository for preventing bribery, corruption, and fraud in energy, AI, and regulated industries through polycentric governance and transaction cost economics.

## Vision

> Corruption is a governance failure. Prevention is a network architecture problem.

This repository treats anti-bribery, corruption, and fraud (ABCF) not as a compliance checkbox, but as a **governance design challenge**. We integrate:
- **Institutional economics** (Commons, Williamson, Ostrom) to understand corruption mechanisms
- **Polycentric governance** to map multi-level enforcement and deterrence networks
- **Transaction cost analysis** to assess prevention investment efficiency
- **Regulatory intelligence** to track enforcement trends and sectoral guidance

## Governance Philosophy

This repository operationalizes a **polycentric, transaction-cost-informed approach** to ABCF:

1. **Polycentric Design**: Prevention is distributed across multiple actors (DOJ, SEC, FCA, boards, auditors, compliance professionals, institutional investors). No single entity solves corruption; governance works through overlapping jurisdiction & incentive alignment.

2. **Transaction Cost Lens**: ABCF prevention is an investment problem. We assess when third-party vetting, enhanced oversight, or external monitoring is justified by risk magnitude—not compliance theater.

3. **Institutional Economics**: Corruption emerges from misaligned incentives, information asymmetry, and opportunism. Prevention design targets these root causes via:
   - Transparency mechanisms (beneficial ownership disclosure)
   - Monitoring networks (regulatory coordination, board oversight)
   - Enforcement credibility (settlement patterns, public prosecution)

4. **Critical Realism**: Regulations are social constructs that work through institutional arrangements. We analyze *how* FCPA & UK Bribery Act actually deter corruption—via incentives, network effects, and legitimacy—not assume compliance simply because law exists.
