# SEC v. Antar — disgorgement reaches losses avoided, not just profits made

**Citation:** *SEC v. Antar*, 97 F. Supp. 2d 576 (D.N.J. 2000).

> **Educational summary — verify against the opinion before relying on it.**
> Pincites are omitted where not confirmed; read the reporter text for the
> court's exact language and figures.

## Why it is in this collection

*Antar* is a workhorse authority for the proposition at the center of this
module's [Core Concepts](../README.md#the-loss-avoidance-doctrine): the equitable
remedy of **disgorgement** in a securities-fraud/insider-trading matter is
measured by the defendant's **ill-gotten gains**, and that measure includes both
**profits made** *and* **losses avoided**. A defendant cannot shrink the
disgorgement base by recharacterizing a defensive, loss-avoiding sale as
"break-even" trading — the avoided loss is itself the unjust enrichment the
remedy is designed to strip away.

## Holding / significance

- Disgorgement is designed to deprive a wrongdoer of the benefit of the
  violation and to deter, not to compensate victims; the court's task is a
  **reasonable approximation** of the amount causally connected to the wrong.
- In the insider-trading setting, that benefit is stated disjunctively as the
  **profit gained _or loss avoided_** — the same "gained or avoided" formulation
  that drives the **§ 21A** civil-penalty calculation (up to treble that
  amount). *Antar* applies the loss-avoided limb rather than treating profit as
  requiring a positive booked gain.
- Practical upshot: correctly identifying a trade as **loss-avoidance**
  (a pre-announcement sale, hedge, gift, or pledge ahead of bad news) determines
  the *size* of the monetary exposure, not merely whether liability exists.

## Relationship to the framework

- Reinforces the monitor's **gain-seeking vs loss-avoidance** tagging: the tag is
  not cosmetic — it maps directly to how disgorgement and § 21A penalties are
  computed.
- Complements the classical-theory analysis in
  [Steinberg & Ramirez](steinberg-ramirez-neglected-weapon.md), which documents
  loss-avoidance as the most common classical-theory scenario.

## Notes for verification

- Confirm the exact disgorgement figure and the court's articulation of the

- Cross-check subsequent treatment of disgorgement scope in light of
  *Kokesh v. SEC*, 137 S. Ct. 1635 (2017) and *Liu v. SEC*, 140 S. Ct. 1936
  (2020), which constrain (but do not eliminate) SEC disgorgement; the
  loss-avoided *measure* survives, subject to *Liu*'s net-profits and
  victim-compensation framing.
