# Industry Associations & Standards Bodies

**Summary.** Industry associations and standards bodies supply the **private
ordering** layer of ABCF governance: sector codes, certifiable management
standards, and peer commitments that raise the compliance floor across an
industry without waiting for legislation. They function as governance centres by
turning shared expectations into **auditable, signalling** commitments that
counterparties, investors, and regulators can rely on.

## ICMM — International Council on Mining & Metals

- **What it is:** a CEO-led membership organisation of major mining and metals
  companies.
- **What it produces:** the **Mining Principles** — performance expectations
  covering ethics/anti-corruption, transparency, human rights, and environment —
  backed by validated assurance and disclosure.
- **ABCF role:** commits members to anti-corruption and revenue-transparency
  performance and to independent assurance, extending EITI-style transparency
  into corporate practice (see [`../frameworks/international-standards.md`](../frameworks/international-standards.md)).

## IPIECA — global oil & gas association for environmental and social issues

- **What it is:** the principal global oil-and-gas industry association for
  environmental and social performance.
- **What it produces:** anti-corruption and transparency **guidance** for the
  petroleum sector, including practical tools on due diligence and revenue
  transparency.
- **ABCF role:** translates high-level standards into sector-specific practice
  for the energy industry that is central to this repository.

## ISO 37001 — Anti-Bribery Management Systems

- **What it is:** the international, **certifiable** management-system standard
  for anti-bribery (see the fuller treatment in
  [`../frameworks/international-standards.md`](../frameworks/international-standards.md)).
- **What it produces:** third-party-audited certification that an organisation
  has implemented an anti-bribery management system, with audit trails.
- **ABCF role:** a **counterparty and procurement signal** — evidence of program
  *design*. Important caveat: certification is **not** proof of the absence of
  bribery and **not** a regulatory safe harbour; regulators judge substance.

## Standards bodies as governance centres

These bodies exercise governance without state power, through three levers:
1. **Codes & standards** — shared, written expectations (ICMM Principles,
   IPIECA guidance, ISO 37001 requirements).
2. **Assurance & certification** — independent verification that creates a
   credible **signal** (ISO 37001 audits, ICMM validation).
3. **Peer commitment & membership** — reputational stakes that make backsliding
   costly among peers.

This is private ordering complementing public enforcement — raising the baseline
and generating signals the rest of the polycentric system consumes (see
[`../institutional/polycentric-governance.md`](../institutional/polycentric-governance.md)).

## Regional cooperation frameworks

- **GCC** cooperation frameworks and national programmes shape ABCF expectations
  across the Gulf, alongside the UAE federal regime and free-zone regulators
  (see [`../frameworks/uae-gcc.md`](../frameworks/uae-gcc.md)).
- Regional bodies and business associations increasingly adopt anti-corruption
  codes and collective-action initiatives that function like sector standards.

## Using industry standards in due diligence

- Treat ISO 37001 certification and ICMM/IPIECA membership as **positive signals**
  in counterparty vetting — but verify substance, not just the certificate/logo.
- Where a counterparty operates in a sector with a recognised code, **absence**
  of participation can be a (weak) negative signal worth noting.
- Fold these signals into the counterparty axis of the
  [risk matrix](../risk-tools/abcf-risk-matrix.md), never as a substitute for
  beneficial-ownership and sanctions/PEP screening.
