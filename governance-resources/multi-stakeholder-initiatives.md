# Multi-Stakeholder ABCF Initiatives

**Summary.** Between national law and private compliance sits a layer of
**multi-stakeholder initiatives** — coalitions of governments, companies, and
civil society that produce transparency data, peer assessments, and shared
standards. In polycentric terms these are the *connective centres*: they don't
prosecute, but they generate the information and legitimacy that let every other
centre (regulators, investors, journalists, counterparties) act. This file maps
the principal initiatives and what each contributes.

## EITI — Extractive Industries Transparency Initiative

- **What it is:** a global standard implemented by member countries, governed by
  a multi-stakeholder board (governments, companies, civil society).
- **What it produces:** disclosure of the payment/revenue flows between
  extractive companies and states, contract and licence transparency, and
  **beneficial-ownership** disclosure of extractive licence-holders.
- **Why it matters for ABCF:** it attacks corruption at the highest-risk
  interface — state resource revenues — through **transparency infrastructure**
  rather than prosecution, and is a primary data source for energy-sector risk
  assessment (see [`../institutional/beneficial-ownership.md`](../institutional/beneficial-ownership.md)).
- **Governance role:** validation/assurance of member countries creates peer
  accountability; civil-society participation is a designed check.

## Transparency International (TI)

- **What it is:** the leading anti-corruption civil-society organisation, with
  national chapters.
- **What it produces:** the **Corruption Perceptions Index (CPI)**, the Bribe
  Payers/Exporting-corruption analyses, and sector and thematic research.
- **Why it matters for ABCF:** CPI is the standard first-pass jurisdiction
  screen (see [`../risk-tools/cpi-integration.md`](../risk-tools/cpi-integration.md));
  TI research shapes the norms and public pressure that make enforcement
  credible.

## UNCAC Implementation Review Mechanism (IRM)

- **What it is:** the peer-review process under the UN Convention Against
  Corruption.
- **What it produces:** country reviews assessing implementation of UNCAC's
  criminalisation, prevention, asset-recovery, and cooperation provisions.
- **Why it matters for ABCF:** it harmonises national regimes and provides a
  benchmark for a jurisdiction's anti-corruption maturity — useful context for
  jurisdiction-risk scoring (see [`../frameworks/international-standards.md`](../frameworks/international-standards.md)).

## World Bank governance indicators

- **What it is:** the **Worldwide Governance Indicators (WGI)** and related
  measures (Control of Corruption, Rule of Law, Regulatory Quality, etc.), plus
  the World Bank Group's **sanctions/debarment** system for its own projects.
- **What it produces:** cross-country governance measures and a public
  **debarment list** of firms/individuals excluded from Bank-financed contracts.
- **Why it matters for ABCF:** WGI triangulates CPI for jurisdiction risk;
  the debarment list is a direct screening source (see
  [`../risk-tools/due-diligence-checklists.md`](../risk-tools/due-diligence-checklists.md)).

## OECD Working Group on Bribery (WGB)

- **What it is:** the peer-review body under the OECD Anti-Bribery Convention.
- **What it produces:** phase reports rating each party's implementation and
  **enforcement** of foreign-bribery laws — publicly naming under-enforcers.
- **Why it matters for ABCF:** it is one of the few mechanisms that holds
  *enforcers* accountable, sustaining the credibility of the whole system.

## How these initiatives function as governance

None of these bodies can imprison or fine. Their power is **informational and
reputational**:
- they **generate data** (CPI, WGI, EITI disclosures) that make detection
  possible;
- they **assess peers** (UNCAC IRM, OECD WGB) to sustain enforcement credibility;
- they **convene stakeholders** (EITI's tripartite governance) so that
  government, industry, and civil society check one another.

This is the polycentric thesis at the international scale: prevention emerges
from overlapping informational and reputational centres, not from a single
global enforcer. See [`../institutional/polycentric-governance.md`](../institutional/polycentric-governance.md).
