# Third-Party Vetting Lifecycle

**Summary.** Third parties are the primary ABCF exposure vector (see
[`../institutional/vendor-third-party-risk.md`](../institutional/vendor-third-party-risk.md)),
so vetting is not a one-time gate but a **lifecycle**: risk-based screening at
intake, contracting that shifts detection to the informed party, ongoing
monitoring, and disciplined escalation. This file describes the four-stage
lifecycle referenced in the README and ties each stage to the repository's
tools.

## Stage 1 — Pre-engagement screening

**Goal:** know who you are dealing with and how risky the engagement is before
committing.

- Confirm identity and ownership; capture beneficial owners.
- Run the **sanctions/restricted-party** screen (OFAC incl. 50% rule, BIS Entity
  List, UN/EU/UK) and the **integrity** screen (PEP, adverse media, debarment).
- Assign a **risk tier** using the [risk matrix](../risk-tools/abcf-risk-matrix.md)
  and the [initial-screening checklist](../risk-tools/due-diligence-checklists.md).
- Escalate to **enhanced due diligence** where the tier, a strong red flag, or a
  state/SOE-plus-intermediary structure requires it.

The output is a documented risk rating and a proceed / EDD / escalate / decline
decision — the start of the defensible selection record.

## Stage 2 — Questionnaire & declaration

**Goal:** obtain the counterparty's own disclosures and commitments, and create
contractual levers.

- **Anti-corruption questionnaire** — ownership, government relationships, PEP
  connections, prior investigations/enforcement, conflicts of interest,
  sub-contracting.
- **Declarations** — confirmation of accuracy; disclosure of any official nexus.
- **Contractual controls** — anti-corruption **representations & warranties**,
  **audit rights**, no-payment-to-third-parties/jurisdictions, no undisclosed
  sub-agents, right to terminate for breach, and training/certification
  obligations (see [`board-management-certification.md`](board-management-certification.md)).

Refusal to complete the questionnaire, sign the reps, or accept audit rights is
itself a red flag (see [`../risk-tools/red-flag-indicators.md`](../risk-tools/red-flag-indicators.md)).

## Stage 3 — Ongoing monitoring

**Goal:** keep the risk picture current for the life of the relationship.

- **Periodic re-screening** at a cadence set by risk tier.
- **Trigger-based reviews** (ownership change, new licence, value increase,
  adverse media, key-personnel change, CPI decline).
- **Relationship reviews** — performance vs. contract; payment/invoice/
  deliverable anomalies.
- **Audit-right exercise** for high-risk relationships.
- **Re-rating** and, on material change, escalation or exit.

Use the [ongoing-monitoring checklist](../risk-tools/due-diligence-checklists.md).
Monitoring records also support FCPA books-and-records expectations.

## Stage 4 — Escalation & remediation

**Goal:** a clear, consistent route from finding to resolution.

- **Findings assessment** — severity, credibility, scope (isolated vs.
  systemic).
- **Immediate measures** — suspend payments, freeze the relationship, preserve
  evidence.
- **Investigation** — proportionate, privileged where appropriate, root-cause
  focused.
- **Remediation** — corrective controls, discipline, contract remedies, and,
  where warranted, self-reporting considerations (see
  [`../frameworks/fcpa.md`](../frameworks/fcpa.md) on voluntary disclosure).
- **Documentation** — decisions, timelines, and outcomes retained.

## The lifecycle as a governance network

Each stage recruits a different centre of the polycentric system: intake
screening uses external data providers; questionnaires enlist the counterparty
itself; contractual audit rights create a private enforcement lever; monitoring
draws on media and regulators; escalation engages internal governance and,
potentially, public enforcers. The lifecycle is how an organisation *builds its
own detection network* around each relationship — see
[`../institutional/polycentric-governance.md`](../institutional/polycentric-governance.md).
