# Board & Management Certification

**Summary.** Certifications, attestations, and contractual anti-corruption
representations convert the abstract obligation of "tone from the top" into
**documented, individual accountability**. They matter for two reasons: they
create *evidence* of a functioning program (relevant to the FCPA compliance-
program factors and the UK Bribery Act "adequate procedures" defence), and they
*shift information and detection duties* onto the people best placed to see the
risk — the polycentric move applied inside the organisation and down the
contract chain.

## Three instruments

### 1. Contractual anti-corruption representations & warranties (RWs)

In agreements with vendors, agents, JV partners, and counterparties:
- **Representations** that the counterparty has not paid, offered, or authorised
  bribes and complies with applicable anti-corruption laws (FCPA, UK Bribery
  Act, local law).
- **Warranties/covenants** to maintain compliance, not to make improper payments
  in connection with the contract, and to disclose government relationships.
- **Enabling rights** — **audit rights**, information rights, no-payment-to-
  third-parties/jurisdictions, no undisclosed sub-agents, **termination** and
  **indemnity** for breach.

These are the contractual levers that make third-party controls enforceable —
see [`third-party-vetting-lifecycle.md`](third-party-vetting-lifecycle.md) and
[`../institutional/vendor-third-party-risk.md`](../institutional/vendor-third-party-risk.md).

### 2. Internal certifications & attestations

Periodic (typically annual) certifications by employees and management:
- **Code-of-conduct / anti-corruption acknowledgement** on hire and annually.
- **Management certifications** by business and finance leaders that they are not
  aware of improper payments, control overrides, or off-book funds in their area.
- **Disclosure certifications** — conflicts of interest, gifts/hospitality above
  thresholds, government relationships, related-party dealings.
- **Sub-certification chains** — senior certifications supported by cascading
  certifications from operating units, so accountability tracks the org chart.

Sub-certification is deliberately polycentric: it distributes attestation to the
people with direct visibility, rather than asking the top of the house to vouch
for what it cannot see.

### 3. Training & acknowledgment logs

- **Role-based training** — deeper for higher-risk roles (procurement, sales,
  agents-facing, JV managers, finance).
- **Tested comprehension**, not just attendance.
- **Acknowledgment logs** — auditable records of who was trained, when, and what
  they attested.
- **Counterparty training/certification obligations** flowed down by contract.

## Why certifications carry evidentiary weight

- **FCPA.** DOJ's *Evaluation of Corporate Compliance Programs* looks for tone
  from the top, training, and accountability — certifications evidence all
  three. False certifications also create individual exposure and a books-and-
  records trail.
- **UK Bribery Act.** The "adequate procedures" defence under **s.7(2)** turns on
  demonstrable **top-level commitment**, **communication/training**, and
  **monitoring** — certifications and logs are primary evidence for the first,
  fifth, and sixth of the MoJ six principles (see
  [`../frameworks/uk-bribery-act.md`](../frameworks/uk-bribery-act.md)).

## Board-level ABCF governance obligations

Beyond individual certifications, the **board/audit committee** should:
- own the **enterprise ABCF risk assessment** and approve the program;
- ensure the compliance function is **resourced, independent, and empowered**;
- receive **regular reporting** on risks, investigations, and remediation;
- oversee **high-risk decisions** directly (large SOE/JV deals, high-risk
  intermediaries, compute/vendor selection — see
  [`../ai-compute/abcf-ai-procurement.md`](../ai-compute/abcf-ai-procurement.md));
- document its oversight — the record that demonstrates governance actually
  functioned.

## Failure modes to avoid

- **Certification theater** — signatures with no underlying testing or
  consequences. Certifications must sit on real controls and real audit, or they
  are evidence of a *paper* program (worse than none). This is the transaction-
  cost efficiency test from
  [`../institutional/transaction-cost-economics.md`](../institutional/transaction-cost-economics.md).
- **Top-only attestation** — the board vouching for what it cannot see, with no
  sub-certification chain beneath it.
- **Stale logs** — training and acknowledgments not refreshed; no re-certification
  at role change.
